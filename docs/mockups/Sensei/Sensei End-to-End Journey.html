04

Is it worth building?

問

The honest case for and against — who it helps, why it's defensible, what has to be true, and the verdict.

得

The value, stated plainly

Two problems most tools ignore: knowledge evaporates between sessions and people, and autonomous work needs a human at the gate. Sensei captures the first locally and lets the Dōjō share it; Relay makes the second answerable from anywhere.

誰

Who actually benefits

  • Solo dev — a memory that persists + free personal governance.
  • Team — conventions taught once, inherited on join.
  • Agency — client confidentiality with an audit trail.
  • Org — measurable first-try-right lift across repos.
盾

Why it's defensible

Local-first + BYOK means no token markup to undercut and privacy as the default, not a feature. The moat is the accumulated, governed knowledge graph — and confidentiality-grade anonymization, which is exactly what regulated and agency buyers pay for.

危

What has to be true (risks)

  • Local observation surfaces lessons good enough to trust.
  • Teams will curate — triage can't become a chore.
  • Realtime through the Dōjō is reliable enough to gate on.
  • The free tier converts to paid team seats.
決
The verdict

Worth building — as one loop, not two products.

The desktop app alone is a nice notebook; the Dōjō alone is a governance console with nothing to govern. Together they form a flywheel: local observation feeds the shared mind, the shared mind makes every developer better, and that pulls more observation in. The combined journey is the product — build the loop, not the halves.

Sequence the wedge: ship the free local loop (observe → learn → personal Dōjō) to seed adoption, then charge teams for coordination (shared scopes, governance, Relay-at-scale, confidentiality). Validate the two load-bearing assumptions first — lesson quality and curation willingness — before investing in the enterprise surface.
